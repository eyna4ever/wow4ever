    Introduction

    Once you have chosen a ruleset, you create your character: faction, race, class, appearance and name. The official guide recommends taking time to read the information on the character creation screen before deciding.[1]

    Key takeaways

    • Faction sets your starting communities, cities and stories; race sets your appearance, starting location and racial abilities; class sets how you play.[1]
    • Nine races and nine classes are available, with new combinations such as Orc Mages or Dwarf Shamans.[2]
    • Your name has two parts, and the full combination must be unique within your region.[4]

    Faction, race and class

    Your faction, Alliance or Horde, determines which communities, cities and stories your character begins with. Your race determines your appearance, starting location and racial abilities. Your class determines how you play: up close, at range, casting spells, protecting a group, healing allies or combining several roles.[1]

    The nine classes are Druid, Hunter, Mage, Paladin, Priest, Rogue, Shaman, Warlock and Warrior. Blizzard advises considering both the class you want to play and the races that can become it; the full table is on the wiki’s “Races and classes: every combination” page (link at the bottom).[2]

    Forever opens new combinations: Orc Mages, Troll Warlocks, Undead Paladins, Human Hunters, Gnome Priests and Dwarf Shamans.[2]

    The races and their history

    The Alliance brings together Humans, Dwarves, Gnomes and Night Elves; the Horde, Orcs, Undead, Tauren and Trolls; the Skyborne can join either. Each race’s history is told in this guide’s three “Race history” pages: the Alliance, the Horde and the Skyborne.[2]

    Racial traits

    In Forever, racial abilities have been reworked to be stronger and more distinct: each race has two active and two passive abilities, tuned to offer similar offensive power while keeping their own utility. Blizzard has detailed a few examples.[5]

    • Dwarves: Stoneform still removes Bleeds, Poisons and Diseases and grants immunity to them, but its defensive bonus now reduces Physical damage taken instead of increasing Armor; Find Treasure can stay active alongside other tracking, Mace Specialization increases critical strike chance with all spells and abilities while using a mace, and Big Game Hunter increases damage against Beasts.[5]
    • Undead: Will of the Forsaken still removes Charm, Fear and Sleep but no longer grants immunity to them; Cannibalize also restores Mana; Underwater Breathing is unchanged; the new Touch of the Grave gives attacks a chance to drain life from enemies.[5]
    • Skyborne, both paths: Walk on Air (glide for 10 seconds), Wind Blessed (passive, 1% Haste) and Elemental Insight (passive, 5% more damage to Elementals); Windshapers (Horde) add Skysight (10% run speed), the High Order (Alliance) Read Ley Line (100% increased Health and Mana regeneration).[3]

    The Skyborne

    At character creation, you choose whether your Skyborne joins the Horde (elemental traditions, access to the Shaman class) or the Alliance (arcane legacy, access to the Mage class). Both begin on Zephras Isle, a level 1–12 starting zone. Skyborne access requires the Heroic Pack or higher digital upgrades.[1]

    Both paths can also become Warriors, Hunters, Rogues or Druids, with druid forms made for the Skyborne.[3]

    Appearance: High or Standard Definition

    For each new character, you choose High Definition models and animations (more detailed) or Standard Definition (the original World of Warcraft look). This choice affects appearance only, not abilities or progression, and can be changed later in the Options or at a Barbershop in game.[1]

    Choosing your name

    Each character has a main name and a secondary name, like a first name and a surname. Two characters can share a first name as long as their full names differ: the full combination must be unique within the region.[4]

    To hide your secondary name in the world: Escape > Options > Nameplates, then check “My Secondary Name”. It still appears in chat.[4]

    Names must follow the in-game code of conduct. The system evaluates both parts together, so splitting a restricted word across the two names will not necessarily work. No name is guaranteed; if yours is taken, choose another combination or try the random name generator. Early Name Reservation is covered on the wiki’s “Getting ready for launch” page (link at the bottom).[4]

    What’s next?

    Confirmed by official sources

    • Nine races, nine classes; the Skyborne choose their faction at creation.[2]
    • Two-part name, unique within the region; the secondary name can be hidden in the world but still shows in chat.[4]

    Still to be confirmed

    • Racial traits: Blizzard has published the full list only for the Skyborne, plus examples for Dwarves and Undead. For Humans, Gnomes, Night Elves, Orcs, Tauren and Trolls, read the information on the creation screen, as the official guide advises.[5][1]
    • Starting zones: your race sets where you start, but no Forever article names each race’s zone; only Zephras Isle (Skyborne, levels 1–12) is announced.[1]
